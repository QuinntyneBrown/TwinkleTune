# Singing Gameplay — L1 High-Level Requirements

**Subsystem code:** SG
**Specification level:** L1 (capability / high‑level)
**Baseline:** v1.0 · **Status:** Approved
**Detailed requirements:** [L2.md](./L2.md)
**Primary dependencies:** Audio Engine (AE), Voice Personalization (VP), Scoring & Results (SR), Coaching & Encouragement (CE), Duet Multiplayer (DM), Grown‑Ups Corner (GC, latency setting)

---

## 1. Purpose

Singing Gameplay is the beating heart of the product: the live "Sing!" screen where a child performs a
song. It presents a synchronised, scrolling playfield of note "pills", moves the child's star avatar to
her live pitch, highlights lyrics karaoke‑style, and captures — frame by frame — how each note went, all
while showering the child with immediate encouragement. It orchestrates the Audio Engine, the singer's
personalized key, and the Scoring subsystem into one continuous, frame‑accurate experience.

## 2. Scope

### In scope

- A single render loop that keeps note pills, the count‑in, the progress bar, karaoke, and the avatar
  synchronised to the audio position.
- Pitch‑driven vertical movement of the singer's star, octave‑forgivingly.
- Karaoke syllable highlighting and phrase cueing.
- Live capture of per‑note performance data (frames observed, frames on‑pitch, first‑hit timing).
- Immediate in‑play feedback: cheers, streaks, a live sparkle count, and the coach bubble.
- A no‑microphone "just for fun" play mode.
- Playback controls within a performance: count‑in, pause/resume, restart, and a practice‑loop mode for
  a single phrase.
- Device microphone‑latency compensation applied to scoring.
- Graceful microphone‑permission handling in the moment of starting to sing.
- Emission of duet score ticks (protocol owned by DM).

### Out of scope

- The scoring mathematics that turn captured frames into stars, sparkles, and a tricky part — **SR**.
- The synthesis and pitch primitives — **AE**; the personalized shift — **VP**.
- The results screen and rewards — **SR / RP**.
- The duet relay, lobby, and opponent result panel — **DM** (SG renders the opponent overlay it is fed).

## 3. Context & dependencies

Gameplay drives one `requestAnimationFrame` loop off the Audio Engine's beat clock. Each frame it
scrolls the note track, reads a pitch frame, positions the star, updates karaoke, and — outside the
count‑in — accumulates scoring frames against the currently active note using the personalized shift and
an octave‑folded tolerance. When the segment ends, it summarises the performance (SR), records the play
(RP), and navigates to results.

## 4. High-level requirements

| ID | Requirement | Priority | Rationale |
|----|-------------|----------|-----------|
| **SG-L1-1** | The system shall present a scrolling playfield of note pills synchronised to the audio, begun by a single tap and preceded by a count‑in, with a "now" marker and a progress indicator. | Must | The core play surface; a child needs one obvious action and a clear sense of "sing now". |
| **SG-L1-2** | The system shall move the singer's star avatar vertically to reflect her live pitch, folding octaves so singing the right note in any octave places the star correctly. | Must | Immediate, legible feedback that "your voice moves the star", without punishing natural octave choice. |
| **SG-L1-3** | The system shall highlight the current lyric syllable by syllable and cue the previous and upcoming phrases. | Must | Karaoke guidance lets a child who half‑reads follow along and anticipate. |
| **SG-L1-4** | The system shall capture, for each note, how many frames it was heard, how many were on‑pitch, and when it was first hit, as the input to scoring. | Must | Accurate, forgiving scoring depends on faithful per‑note capture during play. |
| **SG-L1-5** | The system shall deliver immediate positive feedback during play — per‑note cheers, a streak indicator, a live sparkle count, and encouraging coach lines — and never a negative or failure signal. | Must | Encouragement‑only (P1); the joy of play is the retention loop. |
| **SG-L1-6** | The system shall offer a no‑microphone "just for fun" mode that plays the song fully without scoring, and shall earn no scored rewards in that mode. | Must | A child without mic access (or who prefers not to be scored) can still play; fairness requires no scored rewards. |
| **SG-L1-7** | The system shall provide count‑in, pause/resume, restart, and a single‑phrase practice loop within a performance. | Should | Control and low‑stakes practice keep a child in flow and support mastery. |
| **SG-L1-8** | The system shall compensate for the device's microphone latency when judging which note is being sung, so cheers land in time. | Should | Budget tablets add audible input lag; without compensation, feedback feels wrong and unfair. |
| **SG-L1-9** | The system shall handle a microphone‑permission denial at start‑of‑song gracefully, offering to retry, to play without scoring, or to leave. | Must | The permission moment must never dead‑end the child. |

## 5. Assumptions & constraints

- Layout constant: 90 pixels per beat; the "now" marker sits at 21% of the stage width.
- Hit tolerance is ±50 cents after octave folding; a note is landed at ≥50% on‑pitch frames over ≥3
  observed frames (defined in SR, consumed here).
- Count‑in is 4 beats on a fresh start and 2 beats on resume or practice repeat.
- Slow/practice playback rate is 0.7×.
