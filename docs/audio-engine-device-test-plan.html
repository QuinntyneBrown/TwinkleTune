⭐ Pitch-engine field check

Does TwinkleTune hear the right note?

One page. Three device types. Seven known tones. About ten minutes. The result tells us whether pitch capture is trustworthy and where to look when it is not.

🎯
The only reliable ground truth is a known reference tone.
Do not use a person singing for the accuracy gate: without an independent measurement, we do not know which note the person actually produced.

The answer in one sentence

Known tone in → matching stable reading out.

①Exact note and octave
②Within ±25 cents
③Clarity ≥ 80%
④Stable for 2 seconds

A tone passes only when all four are true in 3 of 3 attempts. The ±25-cent field-test limit leaves useful safety margin inside TwinkleTune gameplay's ±50-cent match tolerance.

Why the octave matters: gameplay deliberately treats C3, C4, and C5 as the same pitch class, but “Find My Voice” stores an absolute MIDI range. A wrong-octave reading can therefore score as correct while still producing a bad voice range.

Step 1

Set up once

1

Use a separate tone source

Use a trusted tone generator, electronic keyboard, or calibrated tuner on a second device. A clean sine tone is best for the baseline.

2

Keep the setup fixed

Quiet room. Source's built-in speaker, test device's built-in microphone. No Bluetooth. Keep them 30 cm apart at 60–70% source volume.

3

Open the diagnostic tuner

Open https://<app-host>/#/tuner and tap Start listening. Grant microphone access. Remote phones and tablets need HTTPS; localhost may use HTTP.

Do not play TwinkleTune's reference audio through the same device being tested. Use the second device so the path under test is speaker → air → test-device microphone → browser → PitchTracker.

Step 2

Run the same tiny script on phone, tablet, and laptop

  1. Silence first: wait 5 seconds. The note must remain —. Any note during silence is a failure.
  2. Play each reference tone for 3 seconds, then leave 2 seconds of silence.
  3. Watch the steady part, not the first attack. Check the exact note, Hz range, clarity, and stability.
  4. Repeat the seven-tone sweep three times. Record the worst result, not the best.
  5. If a tone shows —, raise the source volume once. Record the first failure anyway; sensitivity is a useful signal.

The failure pattern is the signal

What you see What it probably means Look here next
Same tone fails on 2–3 devices Cross-device engine behavior, not one microphone Pitch algorithm, 2048-sample window, frequency gates
Only one device/browser fails Hardware response, browser audio path, or capture constraint difference Device/browser compatibility and actual media-track settings
Wrong octave (O), right letter Harmonic chosen as the fundamental; gameplay may hide it Pitchy's octave selection and voice-range safeguards
Adjacent note or outside ±25 cents (N) Frequency bias or weak margin before the ±50-cent gameplay edge Frequency estimate, device processing, calibration
Dash (—), especially on low notes RMS too low, clarity below 80%, or detector sensitivity RMS/clarity telemetry and microphone placement
Bounces (~) while the source is steady Median-of-five smoothing is insufficient or input is being processed Smoothing, media-track settings, raw-versus-median trace

Best next improvement if failures are hard to explain: add a “record diagnostics” button to the tuner that downloads timestamp, raw Hz, smoothed MIDI, clarity, RMS, sample rate, and media-track settings. The current screen is excellent for a yes/no check but does not retain dropouts or distinguish raw readings from the five-frame median.

Optional, after the baseline passes

One two-minute reality check per device

  1. Sing one comfortable note while watching both TwinkleTune and an independent tuner. They should agree on note and octave.
  2. Complete “Find My Voice.” Repeat it once. Low and high notes should remain within one semitone between runs, and the high must be at least four semitones above the low.
  3. Sing a short song. If the tuner was right but notes land late or not at all, investigate latency/scoring rather than pitch detection.

This reality check finds product problems. It is not a substitute for the known-tone accuracy gate because a human voice is variable.

What this plan is based on

Current shipped behavior

  • The browser requests the microphone with echo cancellation, noise suppression, and auto gain disabled.
  • The detector uses 2048-sample windows, rejects RMS below 0.010, clarity below 0.80, and frequencies outside 70–1500 Hz, then reports the median of five accepted MIDI readings.
  • The hidden tuner shows nearest note, ±50-cent needle, Hz, clarity, and RMS.
  • Gameplay accepts a pitch within ±50 cents for at least half the observed frames and folds octave differences.
