Foundation

Elevation

Things float on the sky like soft clouds. Shadows are blue-tinted and gentle, toy buttons stand on a solid lip, and in the night studio surfaces rise by getting lighter.

Overview

Elevation in TwinkleTune does three jobs:

  • Lift: four shadow levels (--shadow-1 to --shadow-4), tinted with the brand blue in the sky studio so they read as soft light, never grey dirt.
  • Press: the toy lip (--lip-*), a solid offset shadow in the control's edge colour that makes buttons look pushable and shrinks when pressed.
  • Stack: the z-order scale (--z-*), so the bottom nav, dialog scrim, dialogs and toasts always overlap in the same order.

The mocks had seven drop shadows, five rings and several glows. They collapse to four levels, two glows, two rings and three lip depths (D13).

Higher means more important and more temporary. Level 1 sits in the page; level 4 interrupts it.

  • --shadow-glow-accent: the Sing a Song hero tile and the Find My Voice microphone, the two things that most want a tap. In the night studio it becomes a plain dark lift.
  • --shadow-glow-reward: a landed note on the pitch stage glows gold for a moment. The note also turns gold and the syllable stays readable, so the glow is never the only feedback.
  • --shadow-ring-accent: the singer who is singing now (with the text "SINGING NOW ⭐") and today's day disc (with aria-current) (D25).
  • --shadow-ring-reward: the current Find My Voice step and the singer's star while she is on the note.
  • --shadow-focus: a two-ring focus indicator (surface gap, then --color-focus-ring) for parts that cannot use outline, such as rounded inputs inside a well.

The toy lip

A solid offset shadow, no blur, in the control's own edge colour. It is written as box-shadow: var(--lip-rest) var(--btn-edge): the lip token holds the offset and the component supplies the colour. Pressing moves the body down by the difference and shrinks the lip to 1px, so the button visibly clicks.

Easy
--lip-rest on toy buttons and play buttons; --lip-small on number keys, the active chip and the streak chip; --lip-pressed while held.

Lip colours: blue --color-accent-edge, white --color-border-default, pink --color-gentle-edge, gold --color-reward-edge. A disabled button flattens to the pressed lip.

Surfaces in both themes

In the sky studio, surfaces are white and shadows do the lifting. In the night studio, shadows nearly vanish against navy, so surfaces rise by getting lighter: canvas is the darkest navy, cards are lighter, dialogs and toasts lighter still, and wells sink below the canvas. The mocks had the dark canvas lighter than its cards, which made everything look sunk into holes (D08).

Usage

  • Pick a level by behaviour, not looks: if it scrolls with the page it is 1 or 2; if it floats it is 3; if it blocks the page it is 4.
  • Never stack two shadows on one element or invent a fifth level. A card inside a card has no shadow of its own; use a sunken or subtle background.
  • Only toy controls get a lip. A card or tile with a lip looks pressable and confuses a child.
  • Use z tokens, never raw numbers. A new layer that does not fit the table needs a new token, not z-index: 9999.
  • Shadow tokens are themed: do not hand-write a dark-mode shadow.

Accessibility

  • Shadows are never the boundary. A control's edge must meet 3:1 by its fill or border (--color-border-strong), not by its shadow, which disappears in forced-colours mode and on dim screens.
  • Selection is never a ring alone (WCAG 1.4.1). Rings come with text, a filled shape or aria-current/aria-pressed (D25).
  • Focus uses outline where possible so forced-colours mode keeps it; --shadow-focus is for the few places outline cannot follow the shape.
  • Motion. The press travel and lip change run over --duration-fast, which reduced motion sets to near zero; the state still changes, just instantly.
