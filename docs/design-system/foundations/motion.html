Foundation

Motion

Motion in TwinkleTune feels like a toy: buttons click down, dialogs pop in with a little bounce, Twinkle floats and the clouds drift by. Motion celebrates and guides. It never warns, and it switches off completely when the device asks for less motion.

Overview

The mocks used 41 different durations (D14). The system keeps five for interaction (instant, fast, base, slow, deliberate) and five named for what they time (stagger, progress, pulse, ambient, drift). Four easings cover every curve. Every animation in components.css reads these tokens, so one media query can stop all of it.

  • Press is the fastest thing on screen (120ms). The toy button sinks onto its lip and the tap feels instant.
  • Arrival is springy. Dialogs, toasts, result stars and badges pop in on --ease-spring, overshooting a little like a bouncing ball.
  • Ambient loops are slow and decorative: Twinkle floats, stars twinkle, clouds cross the sky in 95 seconds. They sit in aria-hidden layers and stop under reduced motion.
  • Listening is the one loop that means something. The microphone pulses while Find My Voice listens, and the words beside it say so too.

Press Play on any demo to replay it. With reduced motion switched on in your operating system every demo jumps straight to its end state. That is the system working as designed.

Durations

Pick a duration by distance and importance: small, frequent changes are fast; things that arrive from nowhere take longer. Nothing a child is waiting on takes longer than --duration-deliberate (700ms).

Easings

Four curves. Standard for anything that changes in place, enter for things settling in, exit for things leaving, spring for the playful pop that makes the app feel like a toy.

Ambient loops

The sky is alive but never busy. Ambient loops are slow (3.4s and 95s), low in contrast, behind the content and hidden from assistive technology. They are the first thing reduced motion removes.

Pulse and activity

A few loops carry meaning: Twinkle is listening, or something is loading. Each one is paired with words, so a child who cannot see the motion (or whose device has turned it off) still knows what is happening.

🎤
Keep holding it… 62% ✨
Loading…
The listening pulse (.mic.live, pulse-ring, --duration-pulse) and equaliser (.eq.live, --duration-progress); a spinner always sits beside words.
  • Listening pulse rings outward from the microphone and from the current rung of the voice ladder (.rung.now). The message beside it (Keep holding it… 62% ✨) carries the same news.
  • Cheers on the pitch stage (Perfect! ✨, Yes! 💙) float up and fade over --duration-progress. A note that slips by simply stays blue: no shake, no flash, no sound.
  • The parent gate wiggle (.shake, two 400ms swings) is the only "that wasn't it" motion in the product, and it is gentle: the tapped key wobbles, nothing turns red, nothing locks.
  • Skeleton shimmer and the spinner appear only in grown-up surfaces that wait for the family server.

Usage

Choreography rules

  • Reading order. Stagger top to bottom, the way the screen is read. The big action arrives last of the main content, so the eye lands on it.
  • One star at a time. Rewards pop one after another (.d2, .d4, .d6), never all together: each star is its own little celebration.
  • Never block. Entrances do not delay input. A child who taps before the stagger finishes gets her action at once.
  • Motion follows meaning. Gold pops for good news; pink nudges arrive the same way but never shake. Nothing moves to say "wrong".
  • No flashing. Nothing flashes more than three times a second; the fastest loop (the spinner) is a smooth rotation.
  • Use the tokens. Never write a literal duration or a cubic-bezier() in a component; a literal will not switch off under reduced motion.

Accessibility: reduced motion

TwinkleTune honours prefers-reduced-motion: reduce (WCAG 2.3.3 Animation from Interactions, and 2.2.2 Pause, Stop, Hide for the ambient loops) in two layers:

  1. Tokens. tokens.css sets every non-zero duration to 0.01ms, so transitions finish in one frame and animationend still fires for any script that waits on it.
  2. Components. components.css forces animation-duration and transition-duration to --duration-instant and animation-iteration-count to 1 on every element, so infinite loops (floating, drifting, pulsing, spinning) play once instantly and stop.
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  :root { --duration-fast: 0.01ms; --duration-base: 0.01ms; /* … every duration … */ }
}
@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce) {
  *, *::before, *::after {
    animation-duration: var(--duration-instant) !important;
    animation-iteration-count: 1 !important;
    transition-duration: var(--duration-instant) !important;
  }
}
  • Nothing is lost. Every animated state has a static end state that says the same thing: stars are drawn, bars are filled, the microphone shows its ring and the message beside it updates.
  • Entrances use both fill mode, so with zero duration the content simply appears in place.
  • The pitch stage note lane is drawn by script against the song clock, so it keeps moving: it is the song's timeline, not decoration. Its cheers appear without floating.
  • Test it: emulate reduced motion in the browser's rendering tools and walk Home, Sing and Results; nothing should move except the note lane.
