Foundation

Content

TwinkleTune speaks in one voice: Twinkle's. She is warm, brief and always on the singer's side. The only kind of feedback that exists is encouragement, so every word a child sees has to pass one test: would it make a five-year-old want to sing again?

Overview

Two audiences, two registers, one product:

  • Children (5 to 10) hear Twinkle. Short sentences, concrete words, one emoji, lots of praise, never a problem without a next step. Many cannot read fluently yet, so the words sit beside emoji and big buttons that already say most of it.
  • Grown-ups read the Grown-Ups Corner, the parent gate and the family-server managers. Plain, specific and calm: what happened, what still works, what to do.

Use the domain words everywhere: singer, song, voice range, landed note, sparkle, streak, tricky part, duet room, parent gate, Grown-Ups Corner, no-mic mode. Children see friendlier forms of some (a voice range is "your voice", a shift is "3 notes higher"), never a synonym invented on the spot.

Encouragement only

This is the product's first principle, and it is a content rule before it is a visual one.

  • Praise what happened. Lead with the good: You landed 18 of 24 notes! 🎯, not how many slipped by.
  • Every headline is praise. Results open with Super Singing, Beautiful Singing or Brave Singing. There is no failing result.
  • Offer, never assign. The tricky part is a question: want to practice just that bit?
  • Silence is not a mistake. Nothing is ever said about being quiet, slow or off-key.
  • A nudge says what to try next. That sounds close to your low note — reach a little higher! 🐦
  • Problems belong to the app, not the child. Twinkle can't hear you 💙, not "You didn't allow the microphone".

Twinkle's voice

Your notes sparkled! The “My fair lady!” part was tricky — want to practice just that bit?
Praise, then one specific, optional next step (Results).
  • First person, talking to you. I'll listen to your voice so I can tune every song just for you. Elsewhere the app refers to her by name: Can Twinkle hear you sing?
  • Short. One or two sentences per bubble, about 20 words at most. A bubble that needs scrolling is two bubbles, or one too many.
  • Concrete, playful images. Take a big balloon breath… 🎈, squeak up high like a baby bird, fits your voice like a comfy sweater.
  • YOUR in capitals is her one emphasis, for the idea that the song fits the singer: Every song in YOUR key 💙.
  • 💙 is her signature for reassurance; ✨ for sparkle and celebration; 🎉 for big wins. One emoji, at the end.
  • She uses the singer's name where it is natural: Hi, Ada! 👋, Beautiful Singing, Ada! 🎉.

The grown-ups' voice

  • Plain and specific: This erases the profile, sparkles, badges and voice range on this device.
  • Say where data lives: Badges & streaks stay on this device. Profiles, songs and family high scores live on your home server only.
  • Destructive buttons name the loss (Yes, erase everything, Yes, delete); the safe choice keeps things (Keep my stuff, Keep it, Keep them).
  • Still warm. A 💙 at the end of a grown-up note is fine; jargon is not. Say "family server", not "API" or "backend".

Casing and punctuation

  • Sentence case for buttons, bubbles, labels, toasts and dialog titles: Pick another song 🎵, Taking a breather?, Every singer needs a name! 💙.
  • Title Case for named places and features, which are proper names in TwinkleTune: Find My Voice, Pick a Song, Sing a Song, Duet Time, Twinkle's Tips, My Star Journey, Grown-Ups Corner, badge names (Show Stopper, Perfect Ten) and level titles (Level 3 Fledgling).
  • Song titles as the catalogue spells them: Twinkle Twinkle Little Star, Row, Row, Row Your Boat, Frère Jacques.
  • Upper case only through CSS for small labels (ROOM CODE, the stage label SING TO MOVE YOUR STAR ↑↓); write the source in sentence case so screen readers do not spell it out. The mocks still type some of these in capitals; new markup does not.
  • Exclamation marks for celebration and invitations, one at a time. Questions for offers. An em dash, with spaces, joins a cheer to its reason.
  • Curly quotes around lyrics and song names in sentences: The “My fair lady!” part, Delete “Frère Jacques”?. The ellipsis is one character (…).

Numbers

  • Digits always, even for small numbers: 3 notes higher, Sing 3 songs, 1/3.
  • Sparkles: the number then the word or ✨. 760 sparkles collected, +5 sparkles ✨, 140 ✨ to Level 4!, Together you earned 680 ✨!. Ten per landed note, so they only ever go up.
  • Landed notes: "X of Y", never a percentage or a miss count. You landed 12 of 27 notes! 🎯
  • Stars: drawn as one to three stars, said in words for screen readers (1 star earned). Empty stars are "still to earn". Text ratings use ★ and ☆: ★★☆.
  • Streaks: 🔥 7 in a row! for notes, 3-Day Streak for days. A streak of zero is not shown.
  • Voice range and shift: note names for grown-ups and the Me screen (B3 to E5 — that's 18 notes); "notes higher/lower" for children, never "semitones".
  • Durations: ⏱ 0:29 for song length, 1-minute game for Find My Voice. Find My Voice progress is a percentage: Keep holding it… 62% ✨.
  • Grown-up values keep units: 40 ms, Tempo (BPM), at most 2 MB.
  • Room codes: four capital letters, spaced for reading aloud: K W T M.
  • Days: weekday initials in the streak (M T W T F S S), with the full day name as the accessible label.

Errors: fail soft

Children never see an error. Solo play never depends on the network, so a failed server call falls back to the bundled songs and cached data, and the screen says, calmly, what still works. Grown-ups get a plain explanation and a next step.
