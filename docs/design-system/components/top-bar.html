Top bar

The row at the top of every inner screen: a round back button on the left, the screen's title big and centred, and a spacer on the right that keeps the title truly centred.

Overview

Use the top bar on every screen a child reaches from somewhere else: Pick a Song, Twinkle's Tips, Who's singing?, Duet Time, Find My Voice. It answers two questions at a glance: where am I? (the title) and how do I go back? (the ←). Home, Me, Welcome and Sing do not have one: Home and Me are top-level and open from the bottom nav, Welcome is the start, and Sing uses its own pause control.

  • The title is the screen's only <h1>.
  • The back button always goes to the screen the child came from (usually Home), never to a browser history entry outside the app.
  • The right slot is a .spacer the width of the back button, so the title is centred on the screen, not in the leftover space. A quiet link may take that slot when the screen can be postponed (Later on Find My Voice).

Anatomy

  1. Back buttonAn icon button: 52px white disc (--size-control), ← at --font-size-2xl, aria-label="Go back".
  2. Title.title: the <h1>, Baloo 2 bold at --font-size-3xl (28px), centred, takes the free space (flex: 1). May end in one emoji.
  3. Balancing slot.spacer: an empty 52px box, or .link-quiet.spacer (auto width, --font-size-sm), or .actions for grown-up pages.
  4. Row.topbar: a flex row with 16px gaps (--space-4) and 20px below (--space-5). No background: it sits on the sky.

Live examples on this page use <p class="title"> so this page keeps a single h1; in the product the title is always an <h1 class="title">.

Responsive behaviour

  • The bar spans the 470px screen column at every viewport; it never stretches to the window on tablets or desktops.
  • The title wraps onto two centred lines rather than truncating. At 320px every shipped title fits on one line; a grown-up title that would wrap uses .topbar--compact.
  • The bar scrolls with the screen; it is not sticky, so it never covers content or a focused control.

Theming

The bar has no surface of its own. The title reads --color-fg-default over the sky; the back button reads the icon button's tokens (--color-bg-surface, --shadow-1); Later reads the quiet link's (--color-fg-link, dotted --color-border-strong). In the night studio the title turns light over the night canvas and the back button becomes a raised night disc.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A <header> inside main (so it is not a page-wide banner landmark), holding the screen's <h1> and a link back. See the APG Landmark Regions practice. The back control is a link because it navigates; actions are buttons.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabBack button, then the right-slot link or actions, then the screen's content. The spacer is not focusable.
EnterFollows the back link or Later.
Enter, SpaceActivates an action button.

Focus

After a screen change, focus goes to the <h1> (with tabindex="-1") so the new title is announced; the back button is next. The back button's ring is round (3px --color-focus-ring, 3px offset).

Labelling

The back button is named aria-label="Go back" (the arrow alone has no reliable name); on grown-up pages name the destination, Back to the Grown-Ups Corner. The spacer is an empty <span> with no role. A title's trailing emoji is read; keep it meaningful or wrap it in aria-hidden="true".

Motion

The bar joins the screen's entrance (.rise); the back button squeezes to 90% when pressed over --duration-fast. Reduced motion removes both.

Touch

The 52px back button meets the child target. Later is text-sized; give it at least 44px of hit height and keep it 16px from the title.

Content

  • Titles are the screen's friendly name, the same everywhere it is mentioned: Pick a Song 🎵, Twinkle's Tips 💡, Who's singing? 🎤, Duet Time 🎤🎤, Find My Voice.
  • Title case for screen names a child knows as places; questions keep their question mark.
  • The right-slot link is one word that promises nothing is lost: Later. Never Skip or Cancel on a child screen.
