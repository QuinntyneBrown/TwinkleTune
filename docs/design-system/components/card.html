Card

The white, rounded surface every child screen is built from. A card lifts one idea off the sky (today's goal, the score, a form) with a soft blue shadow and generous 32px corners.

Overview

A card groups one idea on a screen so a child can see it as a single thing: Today's sparkle goal, You landed 18 of 24 notes!, the voice ladder, the name form. Cards sit on the page sky; they are never nested inside another card. Use a tile when the whole surface is a big tap target that goes somewhere, a list row for repeated items, and a dialog when the content interrupts the screen.

  • White card (.card) is the default: --color-bg-surface, --radius-xl (32px), --shadow-2, 20px padding. Home goal, tip of the day, the results score card, the level bar on Me, the Find My Voice ladder.
  • Soft card (.card.card-soft) swaps the white for frosted glass (--color-bg-glass with a blur). It lets the sky show through behind a supporting surface that should not compete with the screen's one action.
  • Centred card (.card.card--center) centres its text, for a short celebratory or waiting message such as the duet waiting card.
  • Interactive card is a card marked up as <a href>: the empty-state goal card on Home (Find my voice) is one link, so the card is the control, not a wrapper around a smaller link.

A card is a surface, not a layout. It adds no width rules of its own: the screen column sets the width, and the content inside sets the rhythm.

Anatomy

  1. Surface--color-bg-surface, corners --radius-xl (32px), soft blue --shadow-2. No border: the shadow separates it from the sky.
  2. Leading visual (optional)A ring, avatar, lamp or emoji at a fixed size (--size-art or --size-avatar) so the text column lines up card to card.
  3. ContentA Baloo 2 heading (--font-size-lg) over one line of Nunito in --color-fg-muted. Padding 20px (--space-5); the goal and tip cards use 16 × 20px.
  4. Trailing accent (optional)A decorative emoji (aria-hidden) or an arrow link. Never the only carrier of meaning.

Variants

Pick the white card unless the surface is secondary to something else on the screen. Never place two different card variants side by side; the screen should read as one family of surfaces.

Sizes

Cards have no size modifiers. They are always the full width of their column; height follows content. Padding is the only thing that changes, and it comes from the composed class, not a size class:

States

A static card has no interactive states. An interactive card (an <a class="card">) takes the shared focus ring and the same hover lift as tiles; it is never disabled, because a card a child cannot open should not be on the screen.

  • Loading keeps the card and fills it with skeleton bars, with aria-busy="true" on the card. Only family-server content (songbook, high scores) ever loads; on-device cards render at once.
  • Empty is not a card state: a card with nothing to say is replaced by an empty state that says what will appear and how to earn it.

Responsive behaviour

  • Cards fill the 470px child column (--layout-screen-max) at every viewport, so a card looks the same on a phone and a tablet.
  • Row-style cards (.goal, .tip-day) keep the leading visual at its fixed size and let the text column (flex: 1) wrap. At 320px the text wraps to two or three lines; it never truncates.
  • In grown-up layouts wider than 640px, cards may sit in a .grid-auto; each card still keeps its own padding and radius.

Theming

The card reads --color-bg-surface, --radius-xl, --shadow-2 and --space-5; the soft card reads --color-bg-glass. In the night studio the canvas drops to --palette-night-900 and surfaces rise above it (D08), so cards stay lighter than the page and the shadow deepens instead of glowing.

/* A card that sits on a tinted panel rather than the sky */
.panel .card { background: var(--color-bg-surface-raised); box-shadow: var(--shadow-1); }

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A card is a grouping, not a widget, so there is no APG pattern for the card itself. Use <section aria-label> when the card is a meaningful region (Today's goal, Tip of the day), a plain <div> otherwise. An interactive card is one <a href> (APG Link); never a card with a click handler and a second link inside.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Tab / Shift+TabStatic card: skipped; focus moves to the controls inside it. Interactive card: focuses the whole card.
EnterFollows an interactive card's link.

Focus

An interactive card shows the shared 3px --color-focus-ring ring at a 3px offset, following the card's 32px corners. Controls inside a static card show their own rings.

Labelling

Region labels are short nouns (aria-label="Today's goal"). The accessible name of an interactive card is its whole text, so keep it to a heading and one line; decorative emoji and arrows go in aria-hidden spans.

Contrast

The card edge is a shadow, not a border, and is decorative: nothing inside a static card depends on seeing the card's boundary (WCAG 1.4.11 applies only to interactive cards, whose focus ring is the boundary).

Motion

Cards enter with the screen's staggered rise (--duration-slow, --duration-stagger apart). Under prefers-reduced-motion they appear in place.

Touch

An interactive card is far above the 52px child target. A small link inside a static card (the tip arrow) is at least 44px (D23).

Content

  • One idea per card: a heading a child can read in one breath (Today's sparkle goal) and one line that says what to do or what she did (Sing 3 songs — just 2 more to go!).
  • Progress is always phrased as what is left to enjoy, never what is missing: just 2 more to go, not 2 songs not sung.
  • Numbers that matter appear as words too (1 of 3 songs done in the ring's label).
