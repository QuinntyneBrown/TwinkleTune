Star rating

One to three stars for a song she has sung. Earned stars are gold; stars still to earn are pale and soft, never crossed out, never red. Every result has at least one star.

Overview

The star rating is how TwinkleTune sums up a song. It is display only: a child never taps stars to rate anything. A sung song always earns at least one star, so the rating can only ever say "good", "great" or "super".

  • Result stars (.stars-row): three big star SVGs (84px) at the top of Results, popping in one by one. Earned stars use .star-shape (gold body, gold edge); stars still to earn use .star-shape--empty (pale --color-mark-idle) inside a span.empty that drops the shadow.
  • Inline stars: the text stars ★★☆ beside a song on Me (.mastered b, gold), on the song board (.board-row) and the duet result (.duet-stars). Same meaning, at text size.
  • Mark stars: the ⭐ emoji under Pitch and Timing on the score card (.mark b), one to three per mark.

No-mic mode has no star rating: the Results screen celebrates the sing-along instead (What a fun sing-along, Ada! 🎉) and earns no scored stars. Do not use stars for difficulty ratings in a way that could be confused with a result; difficulty uses ⭐ with a word (⭐⭐ Medium).

Anatomy

  1. Earned starThe five-point star path with --color-reward-bg fill and a 7-unit --color-reward-edge stroke (round joins), 84px (--size-avatar-lg), with a --shadow-1 drop shadow.
  2. RowFlex, centred, 8px gap. Carries the accessible name (2 stars earned).
  3. Star still to earnSame shape and size in --color-mark-idle with a --color-mark-idle-edge stroke, no shadow. Pale, soft, never an outline-only "missing" star.

Both themes

The heading above the stars matches the count, all celebratory: Super Singing, Ada! 🎉 (three), and still a cheer for one star, followed by the gold Practice the tricky part 🎯 button as an option, not a correction.

Responsive behaviour

  • Three 84px stars plus two 8px gaps need 268px, which fits the 320px screen inside its 20px margins. The row never wraps and never shrinks the stars.
  • Inline stars do not wrap apart: keep the glyphs in one element so ★★☆ stays together at the end of a row.

Theming

The SVG paint lives in CSS so it reads tokens: .star-shape uses --color-reward-bg and --color-reward-edge; .star-shape--empty uses --color-mark-idle and --color-mark-idle-edge. Gold is the same in both themes, so a star always looks like a star; the idle colour darkens in the night studio so a star still to earn stays soft against the deep sky rather than glowing.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

A star rating that only displays a value is one image: role="img" on the row with an aria-label such as 2 stars earned, and every SVG aria-hidden. The mocks put aria-label on a plain <div>, which many screen readers ignore; the contract adds role="img". It is not the APG radio-group rating pattern, because nothing is chosen (APG Radio Group applies only to an input rating, which TwinkleTune does not have).

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabSkips the rating; it is not focusable. Screen readers read it in the flow, after the heading.

Focus

None.

Labelling

Say what she earned, not what is missing: 2 stars earned, not 2 out of 3 or 1 star missing. For inline text stars, hide the glyphs (aria-hidden) and add visually hidden words (2 of 3 stars is fine in a list where the scale needs stating), because ★ and ☆ are announced as "black star" and "white star".

Contrast

Text beside the stars (heading on sky)
Mark labels on the score card

The stars are graphics and their count is always also given in text (the accessible name, and the score card's You landed 18 of 24 notes!), so earned versus still-to-earn is never carried by colour alone. The earned star adds a drop shadow and a stronger edge, a second visual cue besides the gold.

Motion

Result stars pop in one after another (.pop .d2 .d4 .d6, --duration-deliberate, spring easing). Under prefers-reduced-motion all three appear at once.

Touch

Not interactive.

Content

  • Ratings are 1, 2 or 3; never zero, never half stars.
  • The cheer above them names her: Super Singing, Ada! 🎉
  • Never pair the stars with "only", "just" or "try harder". One star is a win; the tricky part is offered as fun practice.
