Reward badge

The badges a singer collects on My Star Journey. Earned badges glow in colour; badges still to earn wait, softly greyed, with a lock and a hint of how to get them. There is no such thing as a failed badge.

Overview

Reward badges celebrate milestones: First Song, 3-Day Streak, Show Stopper, Perfect Ten. They live in a three-column grid (.badges) under 🏅 My badges on Me, and a newly earned one also pops up as a gold .badge-toast on Results. Badges are local rewards: they stay on the device, in the singer's own storage.

  • Earned (.badge): white card, the badge's emoji in a pastel disc, the name in Baloo 2.
  • Still to earn (.badge.locked): the card at reduced strength, a grey disc with 🔒, the name in --color-fg-subtle. It is an invitation, never a penalty: the hint says how to earn it.

Scored badges are never earned in No-mic mode. This is not shown as a lock reason to the child; the badge simply stays waiting until she sings with the microphone. For small status labels such as In your key use a pill; for star results use the star rating.

Anatomy

  1. Disc56px circle (--size-avatar) with --gradient-avatar and the badge emoji at --font-size-3xl. Locked: --color-locked-bg, greyscale, 🔒.
  2. NameBaloo 2, --font-size-sm, centred, wraps to two lines. Locked: --color-fg-subtle.
  3. Card--color-bg-surface, --radius-lg, --shadow-1, 16 × 8px padding. Locked: 62% opacity on the whole card.

Responsive behaviour

  • The grid is always three equal columns with a 12px gap inside the 470px column. At 320px each badge is about 88px wide; names wrap to two lines (High Note Hero).
  • The last row may be short; badges are not stretched to fill it.
  • Badge order is fixed by the catalogue, not by earned status, so a badge does not jump around when it is earned.

Theming

Earned badges read --color-bg-surface, --gradient-avatar and --shadow-1; locked badges read --color-locked-bg and --color-fg-subtle. The badge toast reads --gradient-reward, --color-reward-fg and --color-reward-edge, identical in both themes so a reward always looks golden.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

No APG widget applies: a badge collection is a list of static items. Use <ul class="badges"> with <li class="badge">. The mocks use <div>s with a title tooltip for the hint, which touch and screen-reader users never get; the contract moves the hint into visually hidden text (or a visible line, if the design later shows one). The badge toast is announced through role="status" (APG Names and Descriptions).

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabSkips the grid; badges are not focusable. Screen-reader users read them with list navigation.

Focus

None on the badge. If a future design makes a badge open its story, the whole badge becomes a <button> and takes the shared ring.

Labelling

Each badge reads as its name, then its status in words ("Earned" or "Still to earn"), then how it is earned. Status is never carried by the greyscale or the lock emoji alone. The 🔒 and badge emoji are aria-hidden.

Contrast

Badge toast text on the pale end

The locked card is drawn at 62% opacity, which lowers the name's effective contrast below the measured pair. The name is still in the accessible text, but a sighted child may find it faint; see the open item in Sources.

Motion

Badges pop in one after another (.pop, spring easing, --duration-stagger apart). The toast pops and its lip settles. Under prefers-reduced-motion both appear in place.

Touch

Not interactive. No hidden information depends on hover.

Content

  • Badge names are two or three words of praise: Show Stopper, High Note Hero, Brave Bird.
  • Hints say how to earn it as a fun dare, in the present tense: Land ten notes in a row, Sing three days in a row.
  • Never "Locked", "Failed" or "Missed" in visible copy. The accessible status is "Still to earn".
  • The toast names the badge with an exclamation: 🎭 New badge: Show Stopper!
